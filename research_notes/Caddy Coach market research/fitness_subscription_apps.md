# Fitness, health and premium-subscription apps: lessons for Caddy Coach

All sources accessed 18 Nov 2026 unless stated. Many figures come from press or review sites rather than official pricing pages; this is flagged where it matters. Official pricing pages were not fetched for most apps (only Apple's UK Fitness+ page and Peloton's UK app page show up in results), so **check prices on the live UK sites before quoting them**.

## 1. What each app does and its main selling point

### Takeaway
The winners each own one clear job: Strava (social record of your sport), Whoop/Oura (a daily body score), Garmin (free data from the hardware you bought), Runna/Freeletics (a plan that adapts to you), Future/Ladder (a human or coach-led programme), Peloton/Apple/NTC/Zwift (content or a game to train with), Calm/Headspace (a daily mental-health habit).

### Cited Findings
- **Strava**: "the app for active people", with 180m+ users in 185+ countries (company-reported, 2025 Year in Sport) — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025); [Endurance Sportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/?p=221014)
- **Strava bought Runna in April 2025.** The two apps still run separately, but one annual bundle covers both — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-announces-acquisition-of-personalized-training-app-runna/); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/fitness/strava-and-runna-launch-joint-subscription-heres-what-you-need-to-know)
- **Whoop**: a screenless band sold only as a membership. In May 2025 it moved to three tiers (One, Peak, Life) with Whoop 5.0 and the MG device; Life includes the MG with medical-grade ECG — [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/beauty-wellness/wearables/whoop-5-0-review); [T3](https://www.t3.com/active/fitness-trackers/did-whoop-just-win-the-wearable-subscription-game)
- **Garmin Connect**: a free companion app for Garmin hardware. Connect+ was added as a paid tier in March 2025 — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-revolt-over-new-subscription-service-we-need-to-take-a-firm-stand)
- **Oura**: a smart ring (Ring 4 from £349 at launch) plus a monthly membership — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/health-fitness/oura-ring-4-latest-news-rumors-and-what-we-want-to-see); [GB News](https://www.gbnews.com/tech/oura-ring-4-online-sale-currys-boots-amazon-uk)
- **MyFitnessPal**: calorie and macro logging, with Premium and Premium+ tiers — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/myfitnesspal-review); [MyFitnessPal Premium+](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/premium-plus)
- **Freeletics**: bodyweight/HIIT workouts with an "AI Coach" that builds sessions and adjusts them from user feedback. The full coach needs a subscription; a limited free version exists — [findyouredge](https://www.findyouredge.app/news/freeletics-alternatives-uk-2026); [App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/freeletics/id654810212)
- **Nike Training Club**: free workouts. Former Premium programmes were made free to Nike Members — [findyouredge](https://www.findyouredge.app/news/nike-training-club-alternatives-uk-2026); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/apple-fitness-plus-vs-nike-training-club)
- **Apple Fitness+**: instructor-led video workouts tied into Apple Watch — [Apple UK](https://www.apple.com/uk/apple-fitness-plus/)
- **Peloton app**: App One covers strength, yoga, meditation, outdoor running and gym workouts, plus 3 cardio-equipment classes a month. App+ adds unlimited cardio classes — [Peloton UK](https://onepeloton.com/en-GB/app)
- **Future**: pairs each member with a dedicated human coach, who uses AI behind the scenes. Future reportedly dropped an AI-only personal-training product to commit to human coaches — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/future-app-review); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/future-pulls-the-plug-on-ai-personal-training-commits-to-human-coaches/)
- **Ladder**: coach-designed programmes for a whole "team" of members, not customised per person — [Ladder](https://offer.joinladder.com); [Trainwell comparison](https://www.trainwell.net/blog/trainwell-vs-ladder-best-personal-training-apps-compared-2026)
- **Zwift**: a virtual world for indoor cycling and running. "Zwift Plus" launched on 29 October (year per review source) at £26.99/month — [Zwift UK](https://www.zwift.com/uk/news/33635-how-much-does-zwift-cost-and-whats-included-everything-you-need-to-know); [Velora Cycling](https://veloracycling.com/guides/buying/best-indoor-cycling-apps-2026)
- **Calm / Headspace**: meditation and sleep subscriptions — [themindwanders](https://themindwanders.com/headspace-vs-calm/)

### Inferences
- The model closest to Caddy Coach is Runna: an adaptive plan that sits on top of a social or logging layer (Strava). Future/Ladder show a premium tier above that, with a human coach.

### Gaps
- No official 2025–26 user or subscriber numbers found for Whoop, Oura, Runna, Freeletics, Future, Ladder, Zwift, Calm or Headspace.

## 2. Most innovative features

### Takeaway
The standout features turn raw data into one simple daily number or verdict (Whoop recovery and strain, Garmin Body Battery, Oura scores, Whoop Age), or turn activity into a social game (Strava segments, KOMs, clubs and Year in Sport; Zwift).

### Cited Findings
- **Strava segments**: 7.6m KOM/QOMs or course records were set in 2025. Kudos hit 14bn in 2025, up 20% on 2024 — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025); [Endurance Sportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/?p=221014)
- **Strava clubs**: new clubs roughly quadrupled to 1m in total in 2025. Hiking clubs grew 5.8x, running clubs 3.5x, and club-organised events 1.5x year on year. 54% of users track several activity types, and 72% record with the Strava app itself — [Endurance Sportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/?p=221014); [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/consumer/gen-z-chooses-fitness-over-social-media-strava-study-reveals/118471.article)
- **Strava Year in Sport** is now in its 12th year (data window 1 Sep 2024 to 30 Aug 2025). It works as a press and shareable trend report, with brand data such as the most-logged shoes — [Webull/press release](https://www.webull.com/news/13961203642123264); [T3](https://www.t3.com/active/running/strava-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025)
- **Whoop Healthspan** calculates a "WHOOP Age" and "Pace of Aging" each week, with personalised tips. Sources disagree on whether it is in every tier or only Peak and above — [Plunge](https://plunge.com/blogs/blog/whoop-launches-5-0-and-mg-devices); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2242774/most-expensive-whoop-membership-life-why-pay/)
- **Whoop Peak** includes Healthspan, Stress Monitor and Health Monitor. **Whoop Life** adds medical-grade ECG via the MG — [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/beauty-wellness/wearables/whoop-5-0-review)
- **Garmin Connect+** at launch: Active Intelligence (AI insights), a Performance Dashboard, Live Activity, training guidance, expanded LiveTrack and social features — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-revolt-over-new-subscription-service-we-need-to-take-a-firm-stand); [nextpit](https://www.nextpit.com/news/garmin-connect-plus-which-ai-features-worth-it-or-not)
- **Peloton** introduced AI-powered coaching features alongside an equipment refresh and price rises — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/exercise-equipment/peloton-revamps-loads-of-its-fitness-equipment-introduces-an-ai-powered-coaching-features-and-of-course-hikes-prices)
- **Freeletics AI Coach** builds sessions and adapts them from user feedback — [findyouredge](https://www.findyouredge.app/news/freeletics-alternatives-uk-2026)
- **Future**: a human coach backed by AI tools. It reportedly pulled its AI-only personal-training offering — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/future-pulls-the-plug-on-ai-personal-training-commits-to-human-coaches/)
- **Strava + Runna**: AI-powered coaching sold in one subscription — [Advnture](https://www.advnture.com/tech/the-highly-anticipated-strava-runna-subscription-lands-giving-runners-ai-powered-coaching-for-less-than-the-price-of-a-pair-of-running-shoes)

### Inferences
- Golf versions of these: a daily "Game Readiness" or practice score (like recovery); a "Golf Age" or handicap-pace trend (like Whoop Age); "segments" for holes at a home course (a best score on hole 7, with a club leaderboard); a "Year in Golf" share card.
- Future dropping AI-only coaching suggests golfers will pay more for a real coach than for AI alone. A hybrid offer (AI analysis plus an optional PGA pro review) is worth testing.

### Gaps
- Garmin Body Battery and Oura Readiness/Sleep score mechanics were not sourced in this pass. The TechRadar list of Connect+ features would not load (navigation only).
- Runna's adaptive-plan mechanics (how plans adjust after missed or slow runs) were not sourced in detail.
- Zwift's features (worlds, races, levelling, drops) were not sourced.

## 3. What makes users return

### Takeaway
Return drivers fall into four groups. Social feedback (Strava kudos and clubs) and a daily score (Whoop, Oura, Garmin) are the strongest. Plans with a next session waiting (Runna, Freeletics, Future) come next, followed by yearly recap moments (Year in Sport).

### Cited Findings
- Strava social activity keeps growing: 14bn kudos in 2025 (+20%), and 1m clubs. Club events rose 1.5x — [Endurance Sportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/?p=221014)
- Strava's report says "community rules", framing Gen Z as choosing movement over doomscrolling — [Running Insight](https://www.runninginsight.com/movement-is-in-running-is-cool-and-community-rules); [Insider FITT](https://insider.fitt.co/gen-z-drives-strength-social-workouts/)
- Whoop Healthspan is updated weekly (WHOOP Age, Pace of Aging), giving members a regular reason to check in — [Plunge](https://plunge.com/blogs/blog/whoop-launches-5-0-and-mg-devices)
- Adapty: 86.1% of Health & Fitness trial starts happen on Day 0 (install day), with only a 2.6% secondary peak on Days 4–14 — [Adapty H&F benchmarks, Mar 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- The first session matters most. Almost all trials start on install day, so Caddy Coach should show value (a swing analysis or a handicap view) before or right at the paywall in the first session.
- Golf is played weekly, not daily. Practice and recovery prompts, plus a weekly score email or card, may suit golf better than daily streaks.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on how much streaks or notifications lift retention for Calm, Headspace or Duolingo-style apps.

## 4. Monetisation, prices (GBP) and free-to-paid conversion

### Takeaway
UK consumer fitness subscriptions mostly cost £6.99–£17.99 a month or £50–£110 a year. Premium coaching sits much higher (Future about $199 a month). Free tiers stay generous (Strava, Garmin, NTC), and trials are typically 7 days to 1 month. The major backlashes came from price rises and from paywalling features that used to be free.

### Cited Findings
- **Strava (UK):** £8.99/month or £54.99/year, up from £6.99 and £47.99. It was the first rise in about a decade (a ~28% monthly increase), with a messy rollout that Strava apologised for — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-hikes-monthly-subscription-cost-by-more-than-25-per-cent); [Cycling Weekly](https://cyclingweekly.com/news/strava-insists-new-pricing-structure-is-legal)
- **Strava + Runna bundle (UK):** £119.99/year, annual only, bought through Strava. Runna's support page confirms it is rising to £124.99, with the Strava portion unchanged — [endurance.biz](https://endurance.biz/?p=89546); [Runna support](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/18298149-changes-to-our-uk-subscription)
- **Runna (UK):** from 10 Dec 2026, annual goes from £99.99 to £109.99 and monthly from £15.99 to £17.99, applied at first renewal. US pricing is $19.99/month or $119.99/year. The date comes from the5krunner's report of a Runna email — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/11/10/quick-read-runna-price-rise); [Runna support](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/18298149-changes-to-our-uk-subscription)
- **Whoop (UK, annual only):** One £169, Peak £229, Life £349. One source says UK/EU/AU/CA prices rose in Oct 2025, so these may be out of date — [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/beauty-wellness/wearables/whoop-5-0-review); [T3](https://www.t3.com/active/fitness-trackers/did-whoop-just-win-the-wearable-subscription-game)

- Annual-only bundles (Strava + Runna) and partner channels (golf clubs, retailers, as EE does for Calm) are proven routes.

### Gaps
- Official UK pricing pages for Strava, Whoop, MyFitnessPal, Freeletics, Calm and Headspace were not fetched. Treat their figures as press-reported.
- Paywall placement (onboarding vs feature-gated) for individual apps was not sourced.

## 5. What users love and what they complain about

### Takeaway
Complaints cluster around three things: price rises, paywalling features that were free, and broken promises. Users react to the lost trust more than to the price itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Strava:** backlash over price rises of up to 50% in some cases, plus inconsistent pricing between users. Strava moved to one price per country and apologised for poor communication — [BikePerfect](https://bikeperfect.com/news/strava-faces-backlash-as-platform-increases-monthly-subscription-price-by-25-percent); [Cycling Weekly](https://cyclingweekly.com/news/strava-insists-new-pricing-structure-is-legal)
- **Garmin Connect+:** "Furious Garmin users revolt" over fears that free features would later be paywalled. CEO Cliff Pemble said on an earnings call that some existing features might be reserved "for premium offerings". A TechRadar poll of 1,000+ users a year later found free features had not been removed, but fears remained — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-revolt-over-new-subscription-service-we-need-to-take-a-firm-stand); [Advnture](https://www.advnture.com/tech/watches-wearable-tech/garmin-lovers-could-be-forced-to-pay-more-for-favorite-features-as-the-smartwatch-brand-considers-new-paywalls); [TechRadar poll](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/ill-switch-to-another-brand-i-polled-over-1-000-garmin-users-to-see-what-they-thought-of-garmins-connect-premium-tier-one-year-after-its-launch)
- **Whoop (May 2025):** charged existing members an upgrade fee ($49 for 5.0, $79 for MG) despite an earlier promise of free hardware upgrades, which Reddit users called "a slap in the face". Whoop reversed course: members with 12+ months left got a free upgrade or a refund — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/11/fitness-tracker-whoop-faces-unhappy-customers-over-upgrade-policy); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-09/whoop-faces-backlash-after-charging-existing-users-upgrade-fee-for-new-models); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/fitness-trackers/whoop-has-broken-a-promise-on-free-hardware-upgrades-and-users-arent-pleased)
- **Whoop value:** Expert Reviews calls the 5.0 "easy to live with, but not great value" — [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/beauty-wellness/wearables/whoop-5-0-review)
- **MyFitnessPal:** the barcode scanner and deeper macro tools moved from free to Premium — [Recime](https://www.recime.app/blog/myfitnesspal-cost)
- **Strava vs Garmin:** a TechRadar opinion piece calls Strava's rise "reasonable", but the author still won't subscribe because Garmin Connect covers their needs free — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/opinion/strava-premiums-still-worth-getting-unless-you-already-use-garmin-connect)

### Inferences
- For Caddy Coach: never move a free feature behind the paywall. Keep current testers grandfathered, put new features in premium, and give advance notice of any price change.

### Gaps
- No systematic review-mining (App Store star ratings, top review themes) was done for each app. Accuracy complaints (Whoop, Oura) were not substantively sourced.

## 6. UX strengths

### Takeaway
The best UX puts one number or one next action on the home screen. Making content shareable (Year in Sport, kudos) also works as free marketing.

### Cited Findings
- Strava's Year in Sport gets wide press coverage every December (BikeRadar, T3, SGB and others). The recap doubles as a PR and growth moment — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025); [SGB](https://sgbonline.com/stravas-year-in-sport-trend-report-highlights-gen-zs-passion-for-run/)
- Peloton splits tiers by content access (App One gets 3 cardio-equipment classes/month; App+ is unlimited), which makes the upgrade easy to understand — [Peloton UK](https://onepeloton.com/en-GB/app)
- Whoop's tiers each add a clear feature set (Peak: Healthspan, Stress, Health Monitor; Life: ECG/MG) — [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/beauty-wellness/wearables/whoop-5-0-review)

### Inferences
- Caddy Coach's home screen could show a single "your focus this fortnight" card (coach.js already has a two-week focus), plus one simple score.

### Gaps
- No hands-on UX teardown sources were gathered for the individual apps.

## 7. Concrete ideas Caddy Coach could adapt

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced ideas are hole "segments" with club leaderboards, a weekly readiness or progress score, a "Year in Golf" recap, an adaptive two-week plan in the style of Runna, an annual-first price with a trial of 2+ weeks, and a premium human-coach tier.

### Cited Findings (evidence behind each idea)
- **Hole segments and club leaderboards:** 7.6m KOM/CRs and 1m clubs on Strava — [Endurance Sportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/?p=221014)
- **"Year in Golf" share card:** Strava's 12th annual report — [Webull/press](https://www.webull.com/news/13961203642123264)
- **"Golf Age" or progress-pace metric:** Whoop Age and Pace of Aging, updated weekly — [Plunge](https://plunge.com/blogs/blog/whoop-launches-5-0-and-mg-devices)
- **Adaptive plan sold as a bundle add-on:** Strava + Runna annual bundle — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/fitness/strava-and-runna-launch-joint-subscription-heres-what-you-need-to-know)
- **Human-coach premium tier:** Future at about $199/month with a dedicated coach — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/future-app-review)
- **Annual-first pricing:** annual plans grew from 51% to 61% of H&F revenue between 2023 and 2025 — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- **Longer trials:** 17–32-day trials have the highest median conversion (45.7%) versus 26.8% for trials of 4 days or less (RevenueCat 2025, as reported) — [RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [Subscription Insider](https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)

### Inferences
- Other ideas: club or society "teams" (like Ladder's team programmes); partner distribution through golf clubs or retailers (like Calm via EE); a generous free tier (like Garmin and NTC) with premium only for new features.
- A UK golf app could aim for about £7.99/month or £59.99/year, a 14–30 day trial, and an annual-only bundle at launch. This is an inference from the comparables, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No golf-specific conversion data in this pass (covered by other researchers).

## 8. Subscription app benchmarks (retention and conversion)

### Takeaway
Health & Fitness monetises better than most categories: the highest 12-month install LTV ($1.21, Adapty 2026), a median trial-to-paid of about 40% (RevenueCat 2025), and a rising annual-plan share. But revenue is extremely concentrated (the top 10% take about 93%), and renewal retention falls steeply.

### Cited Findings
- **RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025:** H&F median trial-to-paid 39.9%, with the top 10% at 68.3%. iOS converts about 2x Android. H&F had the highest median revenue per install after 60 days ($0.63). Only 5% of H&F apps reach $10k total revenue within 2 years, and H&F has elevated refund rates. (Secondary coverage; not verified against the report itself) — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/fitness-apps-monetizable-winner-take-all-or-most/); [RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- **RevenueCat 2025 (all categories):** 17–32-day trials have a median conversion of 45.7%, versus 26.8% for trials of 4 days or less — [Subscription Insider](https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)
- **RevenueCat 2026 benchmarks:** hard paywalls have a median Day-35 trial-to-paid of 10.7% vs 2.1% for freemium (as reported; this metric is per download, so not comparable to the 39.9% trial-to-paid figure above) — [RevenueCat blog](https://www-docs.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- **Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2026 (H&F article, 27 Mar 2026; $3B revenue, 16,000+ apps):**
  - median 12-month install LTV $1.21 (highest category)
  - annual-plan revenue share rose from 51% (2023) to 61% (2025)
  - about 90% of subscriptions sell at full price (discounts used by ~10%)
  - 86.1% of trial starts happen on Day 0
  - weekly-plan funnel: 9.5% install-to-trial, 42.2% trial-to-paid, 67.7% first renewal
  - top 10% of apps take 92.6% of revenue
  - high-priced annual plans reach a one-year LTV of $70 vs $17 for low-priced
  - UK/FR/DE weekly price index 1.3 vs India 0.3 — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- **Adapty 2026 (all categories):**
  - global install-to-trial 11.2%; install-to-paid about 3.7%
  - renewal retention: 1st 59.2%, 2nd 45.1%, 3rd 37.1%, 4th 31.6%, 5th 27.6%
  - trial users retain 8–60% better at first renewal
  - hard paywalls give 21% higher LTV; soft paywalls give nearly 50% better raw conversion — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- **Older data:** annual H&F subscriptions have a 33% retention rate (Business of Apps, 2023 data) — [Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/?p=98298)

### Inferences
- Charging a higher annual price, rather than discounting, appears to raise LTV in H&F. Caddy Coach should test price, not discounts.
- Revenue is extremely concentrated (the top 10% take most of it). A niche golf app needs a distinct hook and low costs, not a mass-market plan.
