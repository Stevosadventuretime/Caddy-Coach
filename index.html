Live grip check

Face your phone at address. It checks one hand at a time, like a coach: your lead hand on its own first, then your other hand added. A voice tells you which way to turn each hand, and when your grip is right.

What it checks

  • Lead hand turn about how many knuckles show, and where its V points
  • Trail hand turn where its V points
  • Hands together no gap between them, for your grip style
  • Lead thumb covered by the trail palm
  • Hand position between the centre and your lead thigh

Shown for a right-handed golfer, as set in your profile. Grip pressure and finger placement can't be seen by a camera, so use the steps below for those.

What a neutral grip looks likeWhere the Vs should point and how many knuckles to see

From the front, the V between the thumb and index finger of each hand should point between your chin and right shoulder, and the two Vs should be roughly parallel.

Looking down at address you should see 2 to 3 knuckles on your left hand. One or none is weak; four is strong.

Neutral to slightly strong is where most coaches start. Plenty of good players play stronger, but it changes how the face behaves, so change it on purpose, not by accident.

Build your grip in six stepsFrom the left hand in the fingers to grip pressure
  1. Left hand in the fingers. Lay the grip diagonally from the base of your little finger to the middle joint of your index finger, with about a finger's width of grip showing above your hand.Feel: the club held in your fingers, not pressed across your palm.
  2. Close the left hand. Heel pad on top of the grip, thumb just right of centre and pulled up short rather than stretched down the shaft.Feel: the heel pad and the last three fingers doing the holding.
  3. Check it. Two to three knuckles visible, V pointing between your chin and right shoulder.
  4. Right hand from the side. Bring the right palm in facing the target and let the grip run through the base of the fingers.
  5. Fold it over. The lifeline of your right palm covers your left thumb. Join the hands with your grip style (below), with no gap between them.Feel: both hands wrapped together as one unit.
  6. Set the pressure. About 4 out of 10: firm enough to control the club, light enough to let the wrists hinge, and the same from start to finish.Feel: soft forearms, firm fingers.
Choose your grip styleOverlap, interlock or ten-finger
Overlap (Vardon)Right little finger rests on the gap between your left index and middle fingers. The most common on tour, good for larger hands.
InterlockRight little finger links with your left index finger. Secure and popular with smaller hands.
Ten-finger (baseball)All ten fingers on the grip, hands touching. Easy for beginners, juniors and anyone with weak or sore hands.

Your pre-shot setup routineSix steps to the same setup every time
  1. Pick a target from behind the ball. Choose a spot a metre or so in front of the ball on that line.
  2. Aim the clubface first. Set the club behind the ball, face square to your spot, before you place your feet.
  3. Set your feet for the club. Width and ball position from the card above; toes parallel to the target line, lead foot flared out a little.
  4. Hinge and let your arms hang. Push your hips back, tilt your chest over the ball, soft knees, arms hanging straight down from your shoulders. If they reach or feel jammed, move your feet, not your arms.Feel: balanced on the balls of your feet, able to wiggle your toes.
  5. Square your shoulders, trail one lower. Shoulders parallel to your toe line, trail shoulder slightly lower because that hand is lower on the grip.
  6. Look at the target, look back, go. Don't stand over it too long; tension builds fast.

What good looks likeThe positions that matter, from setup to finish

Setup: athletic posture, hinge from the hips with a flat back, weight balanced between heels and balls of the feet, arms hanging naturally.

Spine angle: hold the forward tilt from address through impact. Standing up or dipping changes the low point and the path.

Head: a quiet head, without a big dip or sway, makes centred strikes easier.

Rotation: turn around the spine instead of sliding. Shoulders turn about 90° and hips about 45° at the top of a full swing.

Tempo: tour players average about a 3:1 backswing-to-downswing time ratio, with the downswing taking roughly a quarter of a second.

Impact: hips open, chest over the ball, hands ahead of the clubhead with irons, clubface square to the target.

Finish: balanced on the lead side, chest facing the target, hands high, held still.

Clubface vs path: the face mostly sets the start direction; the path relative to the face sets the curve.

Filming tipsWhere to put your phone for the best analysis

Phone on a tripod or propped at hip height, about 3 metres away, whole body and club in frame. Face-on (facing your chest) measures rotation and sway; down-the-line (behind your hands, looking at the target) measures posture and early extension. Keep the camera still until your finish.

About Caddy CoachHow your videos and data are handled

Body tracking uses Google's open-source MediaPipe BlazePose models (Apache License 2.0), running entirely on your device. Only your trimmed clip is kept while you use the app; saved analyses keep the scores, notes, key-position pictures and skeleton motion, not the video.

Carry is how far the ball flies; total includes the roll. Your clubs and yardages are saved with your profile, so they're there whenever you log in.

Course suggestions and scorecards come from Claude. Rounds and the cards you edit are saved privately to your account.
