# Caddy Coach can win by joining up

Caddy Coach does not need many more features to become one of the best golf apps. It needs to turn what it already has into one weekly loop, and to switch on its best feature. Today it combines on-phone swing analysis, a shot tracer, GPS, an England Golf-style World Handicap System (WHS) handicap, club leaderboards and 30 drills with 3D demos. Rivals sell those across three or four separate apps. Its Claude-powered AI coach is fully built but switched off. The strongest evidence is consistent across sources. Golfers resent £99-a-year paywalls, quit apps that make tracking a chore, and dismiss stats that "tell me anything significant I don't already know". Meanwhile Hole19 and SwingU charge £99.99 for exactly the "round → one focus → drill" loop that Caddy Coach could offer more cheaply and do better, because only Caddy Coach also holds the golfer's swing faults. The recommended plan has five parts:
- switch on the AI coach and build a post-round "One Thing" debrief, delivered alongside a MyEG companion card at the moment every English golfer already opens an app;
- add forgiving, weekly-rhythm habit features borrowed from Duolingo and Strava;
- charge £44.99 a year or £6.99 a month, with testers locked at the planned £34.99;
- sell to societies and PGA pros as low-cost distribution;
- leave GitHub Pages and licence data before taking a single payment.

## The best apps sell one simple job, then make it a habit

Across golf, fitness and learning, the winners pair one small core action with a reason to come back.

In golf, free GPS and a scorecard are now standard, and products compete on what happens around them. 18Birdies passed **10 million cumulative users** and tracked **31M+ rounds in 2025** while keeping games, social and leagues free ([18Birdies](https://18birdies.com/clubhouse/community/the-18-birdies-2025-community-year-in-review)). It charges for "decision" features such as Plays Like distances and strategy ([18Birdies help](https://help.18birdies.com/article/661-whats-included-with-18birdies-premium)). The newest premium job is the post-round verdict. Hole19's Otto writes a report on strengths, weaknesses and where to focus ([Hole19](https://www.hole19golf.com/the-19th-hole/hole19-intelligence-tier)). SwingU Pro links post-round priorities to a personalised drill. Both sit at **£99.99 a year** on the UK App Store ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/swingu-golf-gps-by-golf-genius/id332116103)).

AI swing apps have proved golfers will upload a video for a diagnosis. GolfFix claims **15M+ analyses**, with results in about 10 seconds, and keeps users coming back with daily and monthly reports and "missions" ([GolfFix](https://www.golffix.io/en/golffix)). Mustard has strong reviews but charges up to **£24.99 a month**, analyses only the down-the-line camera view, and is slow ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/mustard-golf-swing-analysis/id6499280777)). Caddy Coach already analyses both camera angles on the phone with no upload wait.

Outside golf, the retention mechanics are precisely measured:
- When Duolingo made a single lesson enough to keep a streak, **day-14 retention rose 3.3%** and new learners on a streak rose 19% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)).
- A "Weekend Amulet" protecting weekend gaps **cut streak loss by 5%** ([Duolingo engineering](https://making.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)).
- About **a third of Duolingo's daily users hold a Friend Streak** ([Duolingo IR](https://duolingo.gcs-web.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8)).
- Strava's social layer generated **14bn kudos in 2025** and clubs quadrupled to 1m ([Endurance Sportswire](https://www.endurancesportswire.com/?p=221014)). Its Year in Sport recap has become an annual press moment ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025)).

The same sources show the backlash risks. Garmin users "revolted" over feared paywalls ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-revolt-over-new-subscription-service-we-need-to-take-a-firm-stand)). Whoop had to reverse a broken upgrade promise ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/11/fitness-tracker-whoop-faces-unhappy-customers-over-upgrade-policy)). Duolingo's "AI-first" memo pushed users to abandon long streaks ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/de/article/news-duolingo-ai-strategy-backlash/)).

**RECOMMENDATION:** golf is a weekly, seasonal sport, so Caddy Coach should copy the forgiving forms of these mechanics: a weekly streak, a Winter Freeze, a partner streak, and effort-based rather than score-based leagues. It should present AI as "built with a PGA pro", never as a replacement for coaches. Full profiles of about 75 apps are in the [competitor analysis](../research/COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS.md).

## Golfers want a verdict, an easy round and a fair price

The pain-point evidence is strongest on price. UK golfers leaving Arccos called its subscription "hard to justify" and "a robbery" ([Golf Monthly forum](https://forums.golfmonthly.com/threads/who-has-moved-away-from-arccos.118414/)). A reviewer complains that "free" apps put up a paywall "within the first round" ([ScoringZone](https://www.scoringzone.net/blog/18birdies-review)). Leavers move to cheaper or subscription-free options rather than giving up tracking ([GolfWRX](https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/2029985-its-time-to-move-on-from-arccos-other-options-for-tracking-rounds/)).

The second recurring complaint is effort during the round: missed shots, editing, putts that aren't captured, and a phone in the pocket. The golfer who stays is the one who says "I basically never miss shots" ([GolfWRX p2](https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/2029985-its-time-to-move-on-from-arccos-other-options-for-tracking-rounds/page/2/)). Battery drain, wrong course data such as Scottish slope ratings ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/thegrint-golf-gps-scorecard/id532085262)), ads and nagging upgrade prompts complete the list. Swing-AI users report grading that is too generous and "not very 3D accurate" results ([GolfWRX](https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/2061178-sportsbox-vs-swing-coach-ai-apps/)).

The UK has one decisive quirk. Since **1 Sep 2025 every English score goes through MyEG** ([Northern Golfer](https://www.northerngolfer.co.uk/new-whs-record-set-in-england-in-august/)). Golfers call that app "glitchy", complain the "Scorecard doesn't show PH" (their playing handicap) and want more statistics ([Golf Monthly forum](https://forums.golfmonthly.com/threads/myeg-app-feedback.115847/)). England Golf does not accept third-party scorecards ([GolfMagic](https://www.golfmagic.com/equipment/buying-guides/best-golf-apps)), so Caddy Coach should be MyEG's friendly companion, not its rival.

Societies still juggle "paper cards, a spreadsheet, an app?" ([Facebook group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/golftrips/posts/4424415841106279/)). The audience is large and growing: **4,199,000** golfers on 9- and 18-hole courses in England ([R&A/EGA](https://ega-golf.ch/sites/ega/files/2026-06/Sporting%20Insights%20Report_The%20RA%20and%20EGA_European%20Golf%20Participation%202025_May%202026.pdf)) and **750,071** club members, up 2.66% ([England Golf](https://www.englandgolf.org/news-detail?newsarticleid=1416)).

The biggest hole in the evidence: **no source broke these needs down by handicap level**, and no golf app publishes churn figures. A short tester survey should come first. Details are in [customer pain points](../research/CUSTOMER_PAIN_POINTS.md).

## Sixty-one ideas collapse into one loop worth paying for

All 61 feature ideas were scored on demand, retention, feasibility and cost efficiency (each weighted most heavily), plus differentiation, revenue and strategic value. The top five are all "switch it on and join it up" moves rather than new builds:
- the post-round "One Thing" debrief (**8.50/10**);
- switching on the AI coach with an editable "what your coach remembers" card (**8.20**);
- 60-second onboarding to a first "wow" (**8.05**);
- a One Focus engine that merges round stats, swing faults and the stated miss into a single two-week focus with before/after proof (**8.00**);
- a MyEG companion card showing playing handicap and a ready-to-post summary (**8.00**).

These are **RECOMMENDATIONS**, based on judgement informed by the evidence. Close behind come the forgiving weekly streak, a simplified Home, plain-English stats compared with golfers of the same handicap (rather than the current PGA Tour baseline), Break 100/90/80 roadmaps, honest camera-angle-aware grading with a published accuracy test, and society mode.

Leagues, tour fantasy, chat and a watch app score lower: they need scale, demand is unproven, or they are expensive to build. The full tables and write-ups are in [feature ideas](../research/FEATURE_IDEAS.md).

**ESTIMATE:** the loop is nearly free to run. A Claude Haiku 5.5 call of about 3,000–4,000 input and 800 output tokens costs 4,000 × $0.10/1M + 800 × $0.50/1M ≈ **$0.0008, about 0.06p** ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). A golfer playing 40 rounds a year costs about 2.5p in debriefs. Sonnet would cost about 20 times as much for the same call, so routine coaching should stay on Haiku.

What makes the loop hard to copy is not the AI prompt but the data behind it. GPS-first rivals have no swing-fault history, and swing apps have no round stats or handicap.

## A £44.99 Plus tier pays back quickly, after the commercial blockers clear

**RECOMMENDATION: keep the free core generous and launch Caddy Coach Plus at £44.99 a year or £6.99 a month.** Use a 21-day trial and a **founding price of £34.99 a year** locked for testers and first-season joiners. £34.99 is the price already coded into the switched-off Plus offer (`app-3.js:1496-1498`).

The grounding:
- UK mid-tier golf apps cost **£49.99–£69.99** and AI tiers **£99.99** ([Hole19](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/hole19-golf-gps-range-finder/id794462227); [TheGrint](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/thegrint-golf-gps-scorecard/id532085262)).
- Higher-priced annual Health & Fitness plans reach a **one-year LTV of $70, against $17** for low-priced ones ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/)).
- Trials of 17–32 days convert best, at a **42.5% median** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)).

The shot tracer and grip check should stay free. Both are on-phone, testers already have them, and tracer reels are free marketing. Plus should carry the new AI value instead.

**ESTIMATE, unit economics per web annual subscriber at £44.99:**
- £37.49 after 20% VAT;
- minus £1.19 Stripe fees (1.5% + 20p + 0.7% Billing, [Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/pricing));
- minus about £1.08 AI for a heavy user;
- leaves **about £35.22**.

An app-store sale leaves about £30.79 after its 15% fee ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)).

At the freemium median of **2.1% download-to-paid** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), 10,000 installs yield about 210 payers and roughly £7,400 a year. Fixed costs of £1,000–£6,000 a year would break even at 29–171 subscribers. Those fixed costs are a scenario: hosting and licence prices were not researched.

Further revenue should come from buyers who bring others with them:
- a **Society Pass** (£4.99 per event or £39.99 per season), modelled on Squabbit's organiser pricing ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1556538444));
- **coach seats** at about £99 a year, against $299–$499 for rival coach tools;
- paid PGA-pro swing reviews. Future's retreat from AI-only coaching suggests people value humans in the loop ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/future-pulls-the-plug-on-ai-personal-training-commits-to-human-coaches/)).

Affiliates are small (about £18 on a £300 driver at 6%), and tee-time booking, hardware, wagers and ads should be avoided.

Two blockers come first. **GitHub Pages forbids commercial SaaS** ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)), and the free weather, imagery and course-data sources need commercial licences. Pricing and economics are in [commercial opportunities](../research/COMMERCIAL_OPPORTUNITIES.md). The phased plan is in the [product roadmap](../research/PRODUCT_ROADMAP.md):
- Phase 1 (now to early January): join up the loop and prove it over winter.
- Phase 2 (January to March): competitive advantage and push notifications.
- Commercial readiness, then a Plus launch around April 2027.
- Phase 3 (advanced AI) and Phase 4 (community) through the season.
- Phase 5: B2B scaling.

## Conclusion

The market research changes the question from "what should Caddy Coach add?" to "what should it stop hiding?" Its competitive edge is not any single feature. It is that one app holds a golfer's swing, round, handicap, drills and friends, and so can say, after every round, the one thing worth practising and why. That is the job golfers say other apps fail to do, and the job rivals charge £99.99 for. It costs Caddy Coach about 0.06p.

**The single most important next build is the AI-powered post-round "One Thing" debrief, with the AI coach switched on beneath it and the MyEG card beside it.** Everything else on the roadmap (streaks, societies, pricing, partnerships) works better once that weekly loop exists. Its success can be measured within weeks on the owner dashboard's 4-week return rate.
